# Discovery conversation guide

Not a form. These are things to ask in normal conversation, in the chair or at the counter, so you can go away, build a demo site and CRM, and come back with something that impresses them. Nobody should feel interviewed.

Rule of thumb: three questions per visit, spread over two or three visits. You already know the answers to half of this from Google, Instagram and looking around.

---

## Before you ask anything: what you can find yourself

Do this the night before. It's most of the brief.

- Google Business Profile: hours, address, phone, photos, reviews. Read the last 20 reviews. Note what people praise and complain about.
- Instagram or Facebook: their best photos, how they announce things, how they take bookings ("DM to book" is a CRM sale).
- Booksy, Fresha, Treatwell or similar: their services, prices, staff names, cancellation policy. All of that is public.
- Walk past: opening hours on the door, price board, signage colours, whether they have a card reader, how busy it is and when.
- Search their business name: any old website, directory listings, wrong phone numbers. Wrong info online is a talking point.

If you have those, you can build the whole demo without asking a single question.

---

## In conversation: the questions and why

Say them your way. The wording here is just a starting point.

### Opening the door
1. **"How do most people find you these days?"**
   Tells you where the site needs to work: Google, Instagram, word of mouth. Also whether they have a Google listing they neglect.
2. **"Do people book through the app or just message you?"**
   Tells you their booking system and whether it costs them per booking. Booksy fees are a sales point.

### Finding the pain
3. **"Do you get many time-wasters, no-shows, that kind of thing?"**
   This is the CRM pitch in their own words. Remember the exact phrase they use.
4. **"What happens when someone messages while you're mid-cut / under a car?"**
   Missed enquiries. The auto-reply and the today-list solve this.
5. **"Do you ever chase people who haven't been in for a while?"**
   Repeat-business automation: MOT due, "it's been 6 weeks". Almost nobody does this, and it's the highest-value feature.

### What they'd want
6. **"If someone built you a proper site, what would you actually want on it?"**
   Their must-have list. Usually: prices, booking, photos, opening hours. Note anything unusual.
7. **"Whose site or Instagram do you rate, in any trade?"**
   The look they like. Saves you a redesign later.
8. **"Who'd be checking messages, you or someone else?"**
   How many logins the CRM needs, and on what device.

### Only if the moment is right
9. **"Have you got a domain name already, or a logo file anywhere?"**
   Saves a step, and tells you whether someone tried this before.
10. **"Are you looking to get busier, or are you full and just want it easier?"**
    Sets what you pitch: more leads, or less admin. Different demo, different price.

## While you're there: things to notice

- Colours and fonts on the sign, price board, uniforms. That's the brand for the demo.
- The exact service names and prices as they write them.
- Staff names, and who seems to run the place.
- How they take payment.
- What's on the walls: certificates, memberships, awards. Trust badges for the site.
- Take a photo of the shopfront if it's not awkward. Ask if you can snap the price board.

---

## What you need before you can build the demo

The minimum. Everything else you can placeholder and fix after they're impressed.

1. Business name, address, phone, hours (from Google).
2. Services and prices (from Booksy or the board).
3. Six to ten decent photos (from Instagram, with their name on the credit).
4. Brand colours (from the sign).
5. Their booking method today (question 2), so the demo shows the improvement.
6. One pain quote (question 3 or 4), so the demo's headline hits it.

---

## The reveal

1. Build the demo on a free subdomain, for example zautoz.highstreetstack.com. Their real name, real photos, real prices. Working enquiry form landing in a demo CRM.
2. Show it on your phone, not a laptop. Open the CRM and show an enquiry arriving live.
3. Open with their pain quote: "You said no-shows kill you. Here's the reminder that goes out the day before."
4. Then: "It's ready. If you want it, it goes on your own address this week." Price: setup plus monthly.
5. Note every "can it do X" they say. Those are the add-ons, and they close the sale.

## Trade cheat sheets

### Garage (Z Autoz)
Ask about: MOT bookings, whether they text people when the MOT is due, courtesy cars. Demo: MOT-due reminder, booking with reg number, "book your MOT" button.

### Barbers (Do It For The Art Studio)
Ask about: Booksy fees, walk-ins vs bookings, per-barber bookings. Demo: their own booking page, no per-booking fee, staff profiles with Instagram.

### Fitness community (Find More Fitness)
Ask about: how people sign up, whether sessions cap out, paid vs free sessions. Demo: timetable, sign-up form, live "18 going" counts like NLB.
