HMRC expense categories for the self-employed (2026/27)

The 14 expense boxes on the Self Assessment self-employment pages, what goes in each, what you cannot claim, and the flat-rate shortcuts.

The short version. Self-employed expenses fall into 14 boxes on the Self Assessment self-employment (full) pages. gov.uk says Making Tax Digital uses the same categories as Self Assessment, so these are the headings to keep your records under.

The basic rules

  • You can claim only costs that relate to the business.
  • If a cost is part business and part private, claim only the business share. A £200 phone bill with £70 of business calls gives a £70 claim.
  • If you use the £1,000 trading allowance, you cannot also claim expenses or capital allowances.
  • Money you take out of the business for yourself is not an expense.

The 14 expense categories

Names and notes below follow HMRC's notes to the Self Assessment self-employment (full) pages. Your software may show shorter names, and some versions group advertising and entertainment differently.

Flat rates instead of actual costs

Simplified expenses let you claim a flat rate for vehicles, working from home and living at your business premises. For 2026/27, a car or van is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles and 25p after that; a motorcycle is 24p. Working from home is £10, £18 or £26 a month for 25 to 50, 51 to 100, or 101 or more hours worked at home. You cannot use the flat rate for a vehicle if you used cash basis and deducted its cost as an expense, or if you claimed capital allowances on it. Our mileage calculator works out the claim.

Under £90,000: income or expense is enough

For Making Tax Digital, if your turnover from a self-employment is under £90,000, you only need to record whether each transaction is income or an expense. From £90,000 you must categorise everything for that source, including records from the start of the tax year. If you are not sure you will stay under, gov.uk advises using full categories.

Keeping the 14 categories anyway means your totals are ready for your tax return, and you can work out your profit with the self employed tax calculator.

Questions people ask

Which expenses can a sole trader claim?

Costs that relate to the business, such as office costs, travel, staff, stock, insurance and bank charges, marketing, subscriptions and training. Money you take from the business for personal use is not an allowable expense. If you are unsure whether a cost is allowable, gov.uk advises contacting HMRC.

Can I claim part of a bill that covers business and personal use?

Yes, claim only the business share. gov.uk's example is a £200 phone bill that includes £70 of business calls: you claim £70.

Do I need to sort expenses into categories for Making Tax Digital updates?

Not if your turnover from a source is under £90,000. Sole traders can then record each transaction as just income or an expense. At £90,000 or more you need full categories for that source.

Can I use flat rates instead of working out actual costs?

For some costs, yes. In 2026/27 the car and van rate is 55p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles, then 25p. For working from home, the monthly flat rates are £10, £18 or £26 for 25 to 50, 51 to 100, and 101 or more hours.
