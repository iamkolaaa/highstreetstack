Renting a chair: are you self-employed?

HMRC's Tax Confident site lists chair renters among the people who usually count as self-employed. Self-employed people decide when and where they work, find their own clients, set their own prices, and deal with their own tax and National Insurance.

Employment status depends on the facts of your arrangement, and we cannot decide it for you. gov.uk has a tool for this, Check employment status for tax.

Tips are income

If you keep tips that customers give you directly, gov.uk says you must report them to HMRC yourself, and you owe Income Tax on them. The Self Assessment notes say turnover includes all payments by cash, card, cheque or any other method, and tips, fees and commissions. So tips go into your turnover along with the price of the cut. Tips paid through an employer are different: those are employment income and are taxed through PAYE.

Costs you can claim, and the box for each

You can claim costs that relate to the business, and only the business share of anything you also use privately. The table shows common costs against the boxes on the Self Assessment self-employment (full) pages. Where the form names no box, pick the box that fits and stay with it.

Questions people ask

Am I self-employed if I rent a chair?

Chair renters are listed on HMRC's Tax Confident site as usually self-employed: they decide when and where they work, find their own clients and set their own prices. Your own arrangement decides it, so use gov.uk's Check employment status for tax tool if you are not sure.

Do I pay tax on tips?

Yes. If a customer gives you tips directly, you owe Income Tax on them and must report them to HMRC yourself, for example on your Self Assessment return. Tips paid through an employer are taxed through PAYE instead.

Can I claim the clothes I wear to cut hair?

Only protective clothing, such as aprons or overalls, and specialist clothing or uniforms that are not everyday wear. Everyday clothing such as shirts, trousers and ordinary shoes is not allowable, even if you wear it for work.

When does Making Tax Digital start for a hairdresser or barber?

It depends on your qualifying income, which is your gross self-employment turnover plus gross property income, before expenses. Over £50,000 on the 2024/25 return: 6 April 2026. Over £30,000 on the 2025/26 return: 6 April 2027. Over £20,000 on the 2026/27 return: 6 April 2028.
