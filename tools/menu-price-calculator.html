The maths, step by step

    How to use the number

    Gross profit (GP) is what is left of the selling price once you take off the ingredients. Pubs and cafes usually aim for 65 to 75% on food. Takeaways and kiosks with cheap ingredients often hit 75% or more; a steak will not.

    The percentage is worked out on the price without VAT, because the VAT is not yours. If a portion costs £2.40 and you want 70%, the ex-VAT price is £2.40 divided by 0.30, which is £8.00. Add 20% VAT and the menu says £9.60.

    The last line in the box, cash margin, is your GP as a share of the money the customer hands over. It is always lower than your GP % when VAT is on, which is why a 70% dish feels like it makes 58p in the pound at the till.

    Assumes every portion is sold and nothing is wasted. Real GP is lower once you count waste, staff meals and portions that go out heavy.
