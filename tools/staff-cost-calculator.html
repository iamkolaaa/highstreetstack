True cost of a member of staff per hour

The hourly rate on the contract is not what an employee costs you. Add employer NI, holiday pay and the pension, then divide by the hours they actually work.

The job

Rates are for the 2026/27 tax year, 6 April 2026 to 5 April 2027. The sums run on the whole year, as an average.

The maths, step by step

    What goes into the real cost

    Employer NI. From April 2025 employers pay 15% on each employee's pay above £5,000 a year (the secondary threshold, £96 a week). These are the 2026/27 figures on gov.uk. Holiday pay counts as pay, so NI is charged on it.

    Holiday. Full-time and part-time workers get 5.6 weeks a year. If hours change week to week, the shortcut is 12.07% of hours worked, which is 5.6 weeks divided by the 46.4 weeks left after leave. Fill in 46.4 weeks worked and the two options give the same answer.

    Pension. Under auto-enrolment you pay at least 3% of qualifying earnings, which is pay between £6,240 and £50,270 a year. It applies to workers aged 22 up to State Pension age who earn over £10,000 a year. Untick the box if the person is under 22 or earns less.

    Employment Allowance. Many employers can knock up to £10,500 off their yearly employer NI bill. It applies once to the whole business, not to each person, and some businesses cannot claim it (for example if the only employee is a director). This page leaves it off unless you tick it, and caps it at this one person's NI, so for a team the saving is shared out across everyone.

    Not included: statutory sick pay, recruitment, training, uniform, equipment, and the apprenticeship levy for larger payrolls. Check your own figures with your accountant or payroll software.
