What you owe on 31 January 2027

The balancing payment for 2025/26, the first payment on account for 2026/27, when you do not have to pay it, and what late filing and late payment cost.

The short version. On 31 January 2027 you pay what is left of your 2025/26 bill, plus (usually) half of that bill again as your first payment towards 2026/27. The other half follows on 31 July.

Work out your own figures

1. The balancing payment for 2025/26

The balancing payment settles your 2025/26 tax year, which ran from 6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026. HMRC works it out by taking the total tax you owe for the year (income tax and Class 4 National Insurance) and deducting any payments on account you have already made. It is due by midnight on 31 January 2027, the same day as the online return.

2. The first payment on account for 2026/27

Payments on account are advance payments towards next year's bill. There are two, and each is usually half of the Self Assessment bill you owed for the previous year, Class 4 National Insurance included. For 2026/27 that means half of your 2025/26 bill. The first falls due on 31 January 2027, so it lands on top of the balancing payment.

3. When payments on account apply

You do not have to make them if either of these is true:

  • your 2025/26 Self Assessment bill was under £1,000, or
  • you paid more than 80% of your 2025/26 tax outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code.

If neither applies, expect them. If this is your first year in Self Assessment, you have not paid on account before, so the January bill is the full balancing payment plus the first payment on account.

4. The second payment, due 31 July 2027

The second payment on account is the other half of the same amount. It is due by midnight on 31 July 2027, so put it aside from February onwards rather than waiting for the summer.

A worked example

Say your 2025/26 bill comes to £8,000. In 2025/26 you paid two payments on account of £3,000 each, because your 2024/25 bill was £6,000.

What you pay on 31 January 2027
2025/26 bill£8,000
Minus payments on account already made (2 x £3,000)− £6,000
Balancing payment£2,000
First payment on account for 2026/27 (half of £8,000)£4,000
Total due 31 January 2027£6,000

On 31 July 2027 you then pay a second payment on account of £4,000. If you had not paid on account before, the January bill would be £8,000 plus £4,000, so £12,000.

These round numbers show how the sums fit together. Your own figures depend on your income, so use the payments on account calculator.

Late filing and late payment

  • Late return: an automatic £100 penalty if your 2025/26 online return is not in by 31 January 2027. Further charges build up the longer it is outstanding, starting with £10 a day after three months, up to £900.
  • Late payment: 5% of the tax still unpaid at 30 days, again at 6 months and again at 12 months, plus interest on the amount owed.

If your income has fallen

If you expect a smaller bill for 2026/27, you can reduce your payments on account online or by sending form SA303 to HMRC; if the bill turns out higher than you said, you are charged interest on the difference.

Questions people ask

What is due on 31 January 2027?

Your 2025/26 tax return and any tax still owing for 2025/26 (the balancing payment). If you have to pay on account, the first payment towards 2026/27 is due the same day.

Do I have to make payments on account?

Not if last year's Self Assessment bill was under £1,000, or if you paid more than 80% of last year's tax outside Self Assessment, for example through your tax code.

How much is the first payment on account?

Half of your 2025/26 Self Assessment bill, including Class 4 National Insurance. The second half is due on 31 July 2027.

What happens if I file or pay late?

A late return costs an automatic £100. Tax paid late brings a penalty of 5% of the unpaid tax at 30 days, 6 months and 12 months, plus interest.
